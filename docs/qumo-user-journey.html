In one page

What Qumo does

A customer buys a bucket at Chicken Licken. On the till slip is a QR code. They scan it, land on a page that looks like Chicken Licken — not like us — give a phone number, and 5% of what they spent lands in a balance they can spend at that same brand. No app to download, no card to carry, no points that mean nothing.

For the brand, it is a subscription. They get their own site on their own subdomain, a console to set up promotions and see what they are costing, and codes to print. They can cancel any month. When they do, earning stops that day and every balance their customers already built stays spendable for sixty days — because the alternative is taking something from people who did nothing wrong.

The same mechanic reskins for the next brand. Campari does not want percent-of-spend off a till slip; they want a unique code under a bottle cap. Same engine, same ledger, different carrier and different colours.

Three things it deliberately is not

  • Not a universal points scheme. Value is closed-loop — earned with one brand, spent with that brand. A shopper cannot move Chicken Licken value to Campari, and neither brand can see the other's customers.
  • Not an app. The chance of a customer downloading something at a till is close to zero, so everything runs in the browser they already have open.
  • Not a card. Identity is a phone number and a one-time passcode. Nothing to lose, nothing to print, and it works on the cheapest handset in the queue.

The shape

Three doors, one accrual path

A shopper reaches Qumo three ways, and only two of them can award anything. The distinction matters more than it looks: a poster is one static code that anyone walking past can scan, so it proves no purchase and must never carry value. A slip or sticker is unique per transaction, which is what makes earning safe.

Every earning door converges on applyAccrual() — one function, one ledger write, one place where caps and stamp-card completion live. Adding a channel adds an adapter, not a second set of rules.

NFC is a carrier, not a door. A tag holding a URL opens the same page a camera would, so a tag on a pack works today with no code at all. What a secure tag adds is the one thing a printed code cannot do: prove the physical thing was tapped. That is why a table-talker can award a visit stamp and a poster cannot — and why every tag still gets a QR beside it, since plenty of phones will not tap.

Earning

What a slip has to survive

Nine checks between a scanned slip and a ledger row. All nine exist now. The last two were the reason unsigned stores could not ship: without a per-person ceiling, a shopper at a store whose point of sale cannot sign controls every field of the URL, and the single-use constraint only stops them reusing a transaction number rather than choosing a fresh one. Twenty fabricated slips minted R1,000 before the ceilings landed — that was measured, not estimated.

Brand

What a brand opens

A separate application on its own host, with its own login, its own session table and its own stylesheet. The console is Qumo's product and looks like Qumo — only the shopper surface wears a brand, because only the shopper is being asked to believe they are dealing with one. A staff member's brand comes from their user row, never from the address bar.

Deployment

Where it all runs

One engine, three adapters, one database — and no runtime connection to CIOS at all. That last part is the change: the earlier plan had Qumo sharing CIOS's database so a shopper's WhatsApp check-ins and their scans could land in one balance. Brand-scoped loyalty removes the reason, and sharing was expensive — seven of Qumo's eight tables held foreign keys into CIOS-owned ones.

Sequence

Order of work

Lettered because it is genuinely a sequence — each phase depended on the one before it. The security work sat before extraction deliberately: those fixes travelled with the engine, and fixing them once beat fixing them in two repos.

Six of the eight are finished. What remains is not held up by the code.

  1. Decide and documentThis page and the architecture note, committed to the repo.
  2. Engine hardeningThree ceilings inside the accrual transaction, opt-out, and the data export. The forgery the ceilings exist to stop was reproduced first — twenty fabricated slips minted R1,000 before they landed.
  3. ExtractionOwn repo, own database. Nothing shared with CIOS at runtime.
  4. Brand identity layerSubdomain resolution, theme fields, and the shell inverted so the brand leads and Qumo is a footer line.
  5. Brand consoleOwn host, own auth, own stylesheet. Overview, promotions, stores, pack codes, team. Settings and analytics still to come.
  6. Subscription lifecycleThe freeze-earning-honour-redemption rule, enforced in the engine rather than the UI. Billing itself is not integrated — this models the lifecycle, not the money.
  7. SMS channelBalance lookup and SMS-to-earn over a rented short code. Blocked on an aggregator account. USSD only if the pilot shows demand.
  8. NFCPlain tags already work — they hold the same URL a QR does. Secure tags add proof-of-presence, a key-management story, and several times the cost per tag.

Unresolved

Blocking, and not yet answered

None of these are code problems. The product runs end to end on a laptop today; what is listed here is what stands between that and a customer using it at a till.

Nothing is deployed

There is no URL. The whole product runs locally — pnpm bootstrap takes a fresh clone to a working install — but nothing is hosted, so there is no link to send anyone and nothing to open on a phone at a counter.

The domain, which blocks the deployment

A brand is a subdomain, so hosting needs a wildcard certificate on a domain we control. qumo.app and qumo.com both resolve to somebody else. qumo.co.za is unconfirmed. Worth separating two decisions that look like one: the domain the business launches on is a branding and trademark question and can take its time, while any throwaway domain unblocks a real URL this week. The root domain is one environment variable, so moving later costs a redeploy.

An aggregator account

The same account serves the login passcodes the shopper flow already depends on and the SMS balance channel. Today passcodes print to the server log, which is fine for testing and useless for anyone else. Worth pricing reverse-billed against standard-rated early: that number sets the per-brand monthly cap, and this is the first feature whose cost scales with engagement rather than staying flat.

Operator or responsible party

Brand-specific sites with per-brand opt-in make "the brand is responsible party, Qumo is the operator" the natural reading — but that is a lawyer's confirmation, and it has to land before the first real shopper registers. Retrofitting consent onto people who already signed up is materially harder than getting it right once. The consent copy is deliberately untouched until this is settled, so it gets rewritten once rather than twice.

Two smaller things, named so they are not forgotten

Rate limiting is per-process, so on a serverless host the effective limit is multiplied by the instance count — it needs a shared store before real traffic. And brand theming has fields in the database and readers on every shopper page, but no console screen to edit them: a brand's colours are currently set by whoever has database access.
