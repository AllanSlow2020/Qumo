The product in a paragraph

A shopper proves a purchase and gets something back. A brand sets the rules and pays for the privilege.

Qumo is a purchase-loyalty platform for South African brands. A customer buys something, scans a code — on the till slip, on the pack, or on a poster — and value lands in a wallet held under that brand’s name. No app to install, no card to carry, no password to remember: a phone number and a six-digit code is the whole of signing in.

The brand it belongs to is the site. chickenlicken.qumo.app is Chicken Licken’s: their logo, their colour, their programme, and nothing about anyone else’s. Qumo is the footer. Brands run their own programmes from a separate console at app.qumo.app — promotions, stores, print codes, team, appearance, plan — without asking us for anything.

Underneath both is one engine: a single immutable ledger that carries points, rand-value cashback and stamp cards on the same table, one accrual path every earn event goes through, and a tenancy guard that makes it structurally impossible for one brand to read another’s data.

Section one

What a shopper can do

Getting in

Land on a brand’s own site Working The brand’s name, logo and colour render on every screen, resolved from the hostname before the page is built. A shopper never sees another brand’s programme on it.
Join from a poster or table-talker Working A single static code that explains the promotion and signs the shopper up. It deliberately awards nothing — a poster that pays out pays out to everyone who walks past it.
Sign in with a phone number Working A one-time code by SMS. No password, no app, no account to create separately. The same number recognised at a second brand needs one tap to opt in, not a second registration.
Stay signed in, safely Working Sessions are revocable and scoped per brand subdomain, so a session on one brand’s site never carries to another.

Earning

Scan a till slip Working The QR carries the store, the transaction and the amount, signed with that store’s secret. A forged or edited slip is refused; a slip already scanned is refused; a stale slip is refused.
Scan a code on the pack Working Unique single-use codes, printed in batches for stickers or under-cap promotions. One code, one award, ever — enforced by the database, not by a check that could race.
Tap an NFC tag Working A tag encoding a normal single-use code URL works today with no change: the phone opens the same page a scan would. The shopper does not have to aim a camera.
Tap a cryptographically secure NFC tag Not built Tags that sign each tap with a per-tap counter, so a cloned tag fails. Needs a /t route and the tag vendor’s key scheme. Phase H.
Earn points, cash back or stamps Working Three currencies on one ledger. A points campaign and a stamp-card campaign differ by one field. Cashback is a percentage of what was actually spent, read from the signed slip.
Get a reward when a card fills Working The tenth stamp issues the coupon in the same transaction that records the stamp. There is no separate job that could fail and leave a full card unrewarded.
Earn by SMS, with no data Not built The slip already carries a unique code. Printing it as text beside the QR — “No data? SMS CL 8842315 to 33xxx” — makes the receipt earn with no smartphone at all. Phase G, and it needs an aggregator account.

Holding value

See the balance and every line behind it Working Balance is always the sum of the ledger, computed on read. No stored total anywhere that could drift from the transactions that made it.
Spend a balance at a till Engine only A two-phase spend — shopper generates a code, staff confirm it — is written and tested, but has no shopper or cashier screen. Parked deliberately: it assumes every till has a staffed Qumo login, which is a large ask of a franchise. Waiting on a real brand to say what their counter can do.
Check a balance by SMS Not built Read-only by design when it lands: a network-supplied number identifies, but does not consent. Phase G.

Rights

Leave a programme at any time Working One switch. Earning stops immediately — the refusal is inside the accrual path, not in the interface — and the shopper can rejoin later.
Download everything held about them Working Their number, every scan, every reward and every consent, as a file. What the privacy notice promises is what the endpoint returns.
Delete everything held about them Not built The harder half of the same promise, because an immutable ledger and a deletion request pull in opposite directions and the answer has to be decided, not improvised.
Read the privacy notice, versioned per channel Working Consent is captured against a version, so a change of terms is a new consent rather than a silent overwrite.
Read the terms of service Not built Blocked on the operator entity — the CIPC name reservation decides whose terms these are.
Section two

What a brand can do without asking us

Running a programme

See the programme at a glance Working Outstanding liability and value issued, side by side — a finance question and a marketing one, and a brand asks both. Plus members, opt-outs, scans and joins over the last seven days, and a warning for any store still running unsigned.
Create and switch on a promotion Working Every campaign starts as a draft. Choose flat-per-scan or a share of spend, choose points, cents or stamps, then activate. Conflicting rules are refused at activation, not discovered later.
Cap the exposure Working Per person per day, per scan per day, and a total budget for the campaign. A brand running five percent cashback has an answer for their finance director.
Pause or close a promotion Working Status changes take effect on the next scan. There is no window where a paused campaign keeps awarding.
See how a campaign is performing Not built Per-campaign analytics — redemption rate, repeat-scan rate, cost per member. The overview answers “what is happening”; nothing yet answers “is this working”.

Stores and codes

Add stores and issue signing secrets Working Each store gets its own secret for signing slip QRs, shown once and stored encrypted. Rotating a secret invalidates old slips immediately.
Generate a batch of print codes Working Unique single-use codes in bulk, tied to a campaign, refused outright against a campaign whose rule cannot award a fixed amount — a bug caught by building this screen.
Export codes for the printer Working CSV with the code, how it should be printed, the URL to encode, and its current status.

The brand’s own house

Manage the team Working Invite staff, set roles, reset a password. Every path that could remove the last active owner refuses — a brand cannot lock itself out.
Change appearance Working Name, logo and accent colour, with a live preview that checks contrast as you type and refuses a colour the shopper screens would render illegibly.
Cancel the subscription Working Earning freezes at cancellation, redemption is honoured for sixty days, then the programme closes. Closure is derived from the date, so no failed job can leave a cancelled programme still awarding.
Sign up and pay Not built Subscription state and its consequences are enforced; the payment side and self-serve onboarding are not. Brands are created by us today.
Section three

What holds it up

Guarantees, not features

One brand cannot read another’s data Working Every brand-scoped query goes through a guard that adds the brand filter itself. A query that forgets it does not silently read everything — it does not compile.
The brand header cannot be forged Working Any incoming brand header is deleted before ours is set. Verified by sending a forged one at both the apex and a wrong brand host.
A shopper and a staff member are different principals Working Two session tables, two cookies, two code paths that never meet. Tested by proving a shopper token does not resolve as staff, and the reverse.
A refused award leaves no trace Working Hitting a ceiling rolls the whole transaction back, so a shopper keeps a slip they can scan tomorrow instead of one silently burnt on an award they never got.
An injected script cannot run Working A Content-Security-Policy with a fresh nonce on every request, so the browser runs only the scripts the server marked as its own. The strict form, not the usual inline-allowed compromise — affordable because there is no inline script anywhere in the app. Verified by driving every screen of a production build and counting zero violations.
The site cannot be reached over plain http Working HSTS for two years across every brand subdomain, so a link that says http never makes the first insecure request. The shopper surface is subdomains, and a brand site reachable over http is a session cookie reachable over http.
Phone numbers are hashed and encrypted Working Hashed for lookup, encrypted at rest for display. A database dump is not a contact list.
Abuse is rate limited, across every instance Working Counters live in Postgres, incremented by a single atomic statement, so concurrent attempts queue rather than interleave and a limit of twenty is twenty however many copies of the app are running. Login attempts are capped per client address, passcode sends and checks per phone number, and guesses per issued code — three limits keyed three ways, because each defeats an attack the others do not.

Running it

One command from a fresh clone to a running app Working pnpm bootstrap finds the database, writes the secrets, migrates and seeds. Verified against a genuinely empty machine rather than remembered.
Seed data for two brands Working Two brands, a staff owner, two stores, a cashback campaign and a stamp card — enough to demonstrate the whole product locally in a minute.
343 tests against a real database Working Across 32 files, including adversarial tests that reproduce an attack before the fix — receipt forgery, tenant leakage, session revocation, and a limiter proven by restoring the old one and watching it fail.
Something tells us when production breaks Working Every server error reaches one reporter: a structured log line always, and a message to a chat channel when one is configured — once per distinct failure per five minutes, so the record stays complete and the channel stays readable. It deliberately needs no database, because the times it matters most are the times the database is the problem.
Deployed where anyone else can see it Not built Runs locally end to end. Deployment needs a domain — the root domain is one environment variable, and subdomain routing is what makes it load-bearing rather than cosmetic.
Section four

How it got here

A Decide and document Done Qumo separated from CIOS: own repo, own database, nothing shared at runtime.
B Engine hardening Done Velocity and per-person caps, liability ceilings, opt-out, data export — each proven by reproducing the hole first.
C Extraction Done The engine moved to its own repo and schema, and was proven to stand on its own.
D Brand identity layer Done Subdomain routing, theme fields, and the shell inverted so the brand leads and Qumo is the footer.
E Brand console Done Its own app, its own auth, its own navigation: promotions, stores, codes, team, appearance.
F Subscription lifecycle Done Cancel any time, and mean it: freeze earning, honour redemption sixty days, close by date.
G SMS channel Not started Balance lookup and SMS-to-earn as thin adapters over the same engine calls. Blocked on an aggregator account.
H Secure NFC Not started Per-tap signed tags, so a cloned tag fails. Ordinary NFC tags already work.
Section five

What is left, and what it is waiting on

Waiting on you

  • A domain. Any registrable name unblocks deployment. The root domain is one environment variable; nothing in the code is tied to a particular one.
  • The Vercel plan. Pro is $20 a month per deploying seat, with $20 of usage included. Hobby cannot be used commercially.
  • The CIPC outcome. Confirming the operator entity changes the operator name, the consent copy and the consent version in one pass.
  • An SMS aggregator account. The same account covers login codes and the whole of Phase G.
  • Operator or responsible party. With brand-specific sites and per-brand opt-in, “the brand is responsible party, Qumo is operator” is now the natural reading. It needs confirming before the first real shopper.

Waiting on a first brand

  • Till redemption. Built and parked. What ships depends on what a real counter can actually run.
  • Campaign analytics. Worth building once a brand can say which number they would act on.
  • SMS versus USSD. SMS first because it is cheaper and simpler; USSD only if the pilot shows the demand.

Just needs building

  • A shared rate-limit store. The one known production gap in the security work.
  • Data deletion. The other half of the rights promise.
  • Terms of service. Waiting on the entity name.
  • Self-serve signup and billing. Subscription consequences are enforced; taking money is not.
The short version

The engine is finished and the shell is finished. What remains is not a build problem — it is a domain, an SMS account, a legal answer and a first brand. Everything on this page marked working can be demonstrated end to end on a laptop today.
