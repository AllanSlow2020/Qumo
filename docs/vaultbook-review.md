# Vaultbook: Competitor Context & Gap Review

**Source:** https://www.vaultbook.co.za (home, platforms, receipt scanning, 4 service pages, case studies, competition T&Cs)
**Reviewed:** 27 October 2026
**Purpose:** Pure context. This document does **not** propose changes to what we have built. It records what the one visible SA competitor offers so we can pick solutions to add *later*, on our own terms, as the product is built out.

> **Method note.** Everything under "What Vaultbook says" comes from their public site, paraphrased. Their numbers are self-reported and unaudited. Everything under "Observations" and "Ideas" is my inference from what the site shows *and does not show*. Not reviewed: their blog, privacy policy, legal notice, About/Team pages, the individual per-campaign T&C pages, and the AFCON case study page. Nothing here is based on using their product.

## 2. What Vaultbook says they do

### 2.1 The five entry channels

| Channel | How the consumer enters | Their stated setup time | Their stated engagement |
|---|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp | Interactive chatbot | ~2 weeks | 100% |
| USSD | Menu-driven prompts (dial a code) | Instant (shared shortcode) or 2-6 weeks (dedicated) | 35% |
| SMS shortcode | Text entry | Instant | 35% |
| Microsite | Web link or QR code | Weeks | 100% |
| Mobile app | In-app contests and games | Months | 100% |

Their own framing: USSD and SMS give **reach** (feature phones, no data) but capture little; WhatsApp, microsite and app give **depth** (full data, richer mechanics). Most campaigns combine channels: simple ones drive volume, rich ones drive data.

### 2.2 Receipt reader and fraud detection
- Entrant photographs the till slip; the system extracts store, product, date/time, amount, receipt number and validity.
- Flags duplicate submissions and manipulated images.
- Product and store rules decide which purchases qualify.
- Auto-corrects image orientation and noise.
- Brand chooses which data points to capture.
- **Not stated:** accuracy rates, which retailers' slip formats are supported, what happens on a failed read, whether a human reviews rejections, appeals process.

### 2.3 WhatsApp campaigns
- End-to-end: strategy, number provisioning (shared Vaultbook number or a dedicated brand number), conversation design, templates, consent capture, fulfilment, reporting.
- Claims partner status lets them onboard a brand to the WhatsApp Business API and get the green tick in days rather than weeks, with a partner route to unstick flagged templates.
- Multilingual flows (English / Afrikaans / isiZulu) where the brand needs it.
- Claims read rates above 90% and a lift in completion from ~20% to ~70% versus SMS-style flows (self-reported).
- Free to the consumer beyond normal data; the brand pays for the conversation.
- POPIA: explicit consent, stored proof of consent, deletion-on-request structure.
- Works across all SA networks and while roaming.

### 2.4 USSD and shortcode
- Positioned as the channel for older shoppers, feature-phone users and data-off prepaid users (LSM 7 and below).
- Use cases: pack-code and till-slip competitions, **loyalty enrolment (name, ID number, product details, in about two minutes)**, quick surveys, voting, service activation.
- Free-to-consumer (brand pays) is the default for promotions.
- Operates gateways into all four networks, presented to the brand as one interface and one report.
- Compliance framed around the WASPA Code (consent, opt-out, pricing disclosure, complaints, record-keeping).

### 2.5 Competition mechanics offered
Giveaways (including referral and list-building), contests (photo, video, writing, hashtag), games (puzzle, memory, word search, hidden object, Minesweeper-style), quizzes (knowledge, personality, product recommender, surveys, predictions), instant-win (spin-the-wheel, scratch-and-win, reveal, coupon codes, share-to-win), multi-stage (tournaments, brackets, prize calendars).

### 2.6 "End-to-end" as they define it
1. Mechanic design and cost-at-volume modelling
2. Channel build
3. T&Cs drafted to CPA Regulation 11
4. Prize sourcing (vehicles, electronics, vouchers, travel, cash)
5. Live monitoring (entry volumes, fraud, network issues)
6. Recorded, replayable random draw (seed and algorithm recorded; optional witness)
7. Winner notification, **ID verification**, prize logistics, press photo
8. Reconciliation and a regulator-ready audit file

### 2.7 Analytics and winner management (listed capabilities)
- Reporting: daily/weekly/monthly timing, hourly entry frequency, entry-pattern analysis, geographic performance by region and store, invalid-entry and fraud-attempt summaries, "actionable insights".
- Winners: random selection, verification, prize-claim management, substitute winners, segmented winner pools, multiple draws.
- Boosting: email, SMS and WhatsApp follow-ups to re-engage lapsed entrants.
- Consulting: channel mix, prize economics, network rate negotiation, A/B test design, post-campaign data re-analysis.

### 2.8 Compliance posture
CPA Regulation 11 (promotional competitions), POPIA, WASPA Code. Competition rules are published on a dedicated page for each campaign and kept online for three years after close. Every campaign gets a document reference number.

### 2.9 Claimed outcomes (self-reported, treat as marketing)
89% retention (entrants keep the same WhatsApp number so the audience stays reachable), +72% viral growth, +24% referral lift, 48 days as the "optimal" run length.

## 3. Observations (my inference from the site)

### 3.1 Where Vaultbook is strong (what we would be measured against)
- **Distribution and trust:** named FMCG clients, WASPA, Meta partner and direct operator integrations are hard to replicate quickly.
- **Reach to feature-phone and data-off users** through USSD.
- **Compliance as a selling point:** CPA/POPIA/WASPA, audit file, recorded draw, three-year rule retention.
- **Fraud and proof-of-purchase handling** is a headline feature.
- **Full-service convenience:** a brand can hand over a brief and get a finished campaign.

### 3.2 Where the public site is thin or silent (candidate "holes")

| # | Observation | Why it might matter |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Campaign-by-campaign, brand-owned model.** Each activation is a time-boxed promotion with its own rules page. The site describes no persistent consumer identity, profile or history across campaigns, beyond "same WhatsApp number = reachable". | A consumer who enters five campaigns has no single place to see entries, wins or status. |
| 2 | **No consumer-facing "my account" view is described.** No entry history, win status, prize-claim tracking or points/wallet shown. | Entrants likely chase status by messaging or emailing. |
| 3 | **Self-serve is absent.** No pricing, no demo, no dashboard preview, no sign-up. Everything starts with a contact form or WhatsApp chat. Reply promise is within one working day. | Slower for small and mid-size brands and anyone wanting to test quickly. |
| 4 | **Setup lead times are long for the richer channels** (WhatsApp ~2 weeks, microsite weeks, app months). | Speed-to-launch is a differentiator for anything non-USSD. |
| 5 | **The "no app" entry is not their strongest path.** Their no-install options are USSD/SMS (low data capture, 35% engagement by their own table) and WhatsApp (needs data and a chat). Their only high-depth native option is an app that takes months. | A fast, low-friction, no-install entry that still captures real data sits between their lanes. |
| 6 | **Receipt reader claims have no proof points.** No accuracy figures, retailer coverage, failure handling or appeals. | Trust and fairness gaps for entrants whose valid slip gets rejected. |
| 7 | **Winner verification is mentioned but not shown.** "ID verification" appears as a step, but the flow, document types, turnaround time and what happens when a winner can't be verified are not described. | Prize claim is usually where entrants drop off or complain. |
| 8 | **Dependency on third-party rules.** WhatsApp's 24-hour window, template approvals and category pricing; WASPA/operator rules on USSD and SMS. | Their flexibility is bounded by Meta and the networks. |
| 9 | **Brand-side tooling is opaque.** Analytics are listed, but there is no sign of a live self-serve dashboard, exports, API or integrations (CRM, POS, loyalty, e-commerce). | Possible gap for brands that want their own data pipeline. |
| 10 | **Entrant data ownership and portability are not discussed.** The site covers consent and deletion on request, but nothing on what the entrant can see, export or reuse. | A POPIA-forward transparency angle. |
| 11 | **Limited public detail on accessibility and language** beyond a mention of English/Afrikaans/isiZulu on WhatsApp. Nothing on Xhosa, Sotho, Tswana and others, or on accessibility for low-literacy or visually impaired users. | Reach in the mass market is language-dependent. |
| 12 | **Marketing site is agency-style, not product-style.** Long prose pages, no product visuals of actual flows, no live demo. One team member's profile link points to the generic Facebook home page rather than a profile. | Our product site (Lovable, later) can lead with the actual product experience. |
| 13 | **Self-reported metrics with no methodology.** "89% retention" and "100% engagement" are not defined. | Credibility gap we can fill with transparent, defined metrics. |

### 3.3 What they validate for us
- Phone-number-based identity is clearly the backbone of their data capture and retention story (the WhatsApp number is the persistent handle). That supports the instinct that a phone number entry point is the right primitive.
- They themselves rank apps as the slowest, heaviest channel, which supports the no-app direction.
- Their USSD page lists loyalty enrolment with ID-number capture in about two minutes as a core use case, so verified identity at entry is something the market already understands.

## 5. Open questions worth answering before we decide

1. Is our primary buyer the **brand** (like Vaultbook) or the **retailer / venue**, and does that change which of the above matters?
2. Do we run **time-boxed promotions**, **ongoing loyalty**, or both? Vaultbook is overwhelmingly the former.
3. Where does identity verification sit in our flow, and when is it triggered (at entry, or only at prize claim)?
4. Which of their five channels do we *deliberately* not support, and how do we explain that to a feature-phone audience?
5. What is our stance on WhatsApp as an add-on channel versus our phone-number entry point?
6. Which claims of theirs would we need to be able to match with evidence (fraud handling, audit trail, draw integrity) to be taken seriously by a large brand's legal team?
